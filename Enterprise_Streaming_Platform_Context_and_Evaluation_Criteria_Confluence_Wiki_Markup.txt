h1. Enterprise Streaming Data Platform

*Platform Context and Evaluation Criteria*
_Global Payments · Enterprise Data Platform · August 2026 · For vendor and technology evaluation_
----

h2. 1. Purpose and background

Global Payments is selecting the target platform for its enterprise streaming data estate: the Kafka-based backbone that ingests every business-relevant event once and delivers it to every approved destination. The platform underpins real-time payments capability across the company, including authorization-adjacent signals, fraud decisioning, settlement visibility, and merchant lifecycle events, and it continuously feeds the open data lake and the operational data store.

Today the estate runs self-managed Apache Kafka (Confluent Platform) on virtual machines across two tiers: a cardholder data environment tier, where card data is tokenized at the edge through the enterprise tokenization service before it propagates, and a non-PCI tier that serves the wider estate. Events arrive from native streaming publishers and from database change data capture. The current estate has no platform-level autoscaling, no formal self-service onboarding, and no multi-region disaster recovery pattern, and it is operated by a small central team.

This evaluation selects the platform on which the next decade of streaming runs. Fully managed, bring-your-own-cloud, and modernized self-managed deployment models are all in scope, and each will be judged on the same criteria: capability, security and compliance fit, migration effort from the current estate, and full-lifecycle economics including the people required to run it. The platform must operate across two clouds (AWS primary, Google Cloud), meet PCI DSS and SOX obligations, and absorb an order of magnitude of volume growth without re-architecture.

h2. 2. Guiding principles

# *Ingest once, deliver everywhere.* A single publish fans out to every approved destination through configuration, not code.
# *Tokenize at the edge.* Card data is tokenized inside the cardholder data environment before it propagates; downstream consumers see tokens, never card numbers.
# *High availability is non-negotiable.* No single point of failure in the critical path; the most critical topics target 99.99% availability or better, with multi-region resilience.
# *Elastic scalability is non-negotiable.* The platform absorbs 10× volume growth through horizontal scale, with no re-architecture and no static capacity planning.
# *Open protocol, open formats.* Kafka protocol compatibility, open serialization, and delivery into open table formats; no capability may depend on a closed format without an exit path.
# *Self-service or it does not ship.* Topic, schema, access, and pipeline provisioning are automated; a capability that requires a manual ticket is a platform failure.
# *Platform as a product.* Developer experience, published service levels, and time-to-onboard are the leading measures of success, ahead of infrastructure metrics.
# *Cost transparency.* Every topic, pipeline, and consumer carries cost attribution; cost per million events is measured and driven down year over year.

h2. 3. What a world-class platform provides

A world-class enterprise streaming platform provides:

* Durable, ordered event ingestion at billions of events per day, from native streaming publishers and database change data capture.
* A PCI-segregated topology: a cardholder data tier with tokenization at the edge and a non-PCI tier, with governed, audited propagation between them.
* A schema registry with enforced compatibility, data contracts, and controlled evolution.
* Stream processing for validation, enrichment, domain mapping, and routing, with exactly-once semantics where the business requires them.
* Configuration-driven delivery to approved destinations: the open data lake (Apache Iceberg), the operational data store, and downstream consumers.
* Self-service onboarding: declarative provisioning of topics, schemas, access, and sinks, with observability and dead-letter handling from day one.
* End-to-end observability: lag, latency, throughput, and freshness per topic, with tiered alerting and lineage.
* Multi-region disaster recovery with defined recovery point and recovery time objectives, exercised regularly.

h2. 4. Evaluation criteria

Vendors and technologies will be assessed against the criteria below. Stream processing is evaluated as a sub-capability of the platform (group E); it may nevertheless be sourced independently, and responses must not assume the broker platform and the processing engine are procured together. Reference volumetrics are provided at vendor briefing; benchmark claims must state method, load profile, message size, and configuration.

h3. A. Performance and scale

||ID||Criterion||Requirement||
|A1|Throughput and headroom|Sustain the reference production volumes (volumetrics provided at briefing) and demonstrate scale to 10× through horizontal scale-out with no downtime, no re-architecture, and no manual partition-rebalancing burden on the platform team.|
|A2|Latency|Produce-to-consume p99 of 100 ms or better for critical topics at sustained load with acknowledged, replicated writes (or the platform’s equivalent durability). Provide benchmark method and behaviour during broker failure, rebalance, and scaling events.|
|A3|Limits and growth pattern|State per-cluster limits (partitions, connections, throughput) and the supported pattern for growing beyond them, including the operational effort each scaling event requires.|
|A4|Retention economics|Retention decoupled from broker compute through tiered or object storage. State the cost model and the latency behaviour when consumers read historical data.|

h3. B. Availability and resilience

||ID||Criterion||Requirement||
|B1|Availability|State the contractual SLA per cluster type and historical attainment; 99.99% or better is expected for the critical tier, with the required topology described. The data plane must survive control-plane failure.|
|B2|Multi-region DR|Cross-region replication supporting active-active and active-passive patterns, including offsets, schemas, and consumer-group state. Near-zero recovery point and a recovery time under one hour for the critical tier; describe client failover.|
|B3|Zero-downtime operations|Rolling upgrades, patching, and cluster changes with no producer or consumer interruption; no maintenance windows for the critical tier.|
|B4|Degradation behaviour|Documented failure modes for zone loss, region loss, storage impairment, and control-plane outage, each with recovery mechanics and blast radius.|

h3. C. Security, compliance, and PCI

||ID||Criterion||Requirement||
|C1|PCI DSS|For managed offerings: current PCI DSS attestation as a Level 1 service provider, with the Attestation of Compliance available under NDA and a shared-responsibility matrix. For bring-your-own-cloud or self-managed models: a reference architecture supporting cardholder data environment scope and audit evidence.|
|C2|Private connectivity|All access over private networking (AWS PrivateLink or VPC peering; Google Cloud Private Service Connect); no public endpoints. Private connectivity from the platform and its processing tier to the enterprise tokenization service in a private VPC is a hard requirement; describe the pattern.|
|C3|Encryption and access|TLS 1.2 or later in transit and AES-256 at rest; customer-managed keys through AWS KMS and Google Cloud KMS; per-topic authorization (ACL or role-based); mTLS or OAuth client authentication.|
|C4|Audit, residency, certifications|Immutable audit of administrative and access events exportable to the enterprise SIEM; EU residency for data, metadata, and backups; SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001; controls supporting SOX.|

h3. D. Ecosystem and integration

||ID||Criterion||Requirement||
|D1|Kafka protocol|Full compatibility with the open Kafka protocol and client ecosystem at current versions; state any API gaps, semantic divergence, or version lag.|
|D2|Connectors|A vendor-supported connector runtime and catalog covering databases and change data capture, object storage, the operational data store, and Apache Iceberg; support for custom connectors; connectors covered by the support SLA.|
|D3|Schema registry|A managed schema registry with Avro and Protobuf, enforced compatibility modes, and a stated migration or coexistence path from an existing Confluent Schema Registry.|
|D4|Open table delivery|State the supported paths from topics to Apache Iceberg tables and the catalog integrations offered (Iceberg REST catalog, Unity Catalog, AWS Glue). The sink engine is a separate decision; no proprietary sink path may be a condition of the platform.|

h3. E. Stream processing (sub-capability)

||ID||Criterion||Requirement||
|E1|Engine|Managed or vendor-supported stateful stream processing (Apache Flink or equivalent): exactly-once semantics, event-time windowing, durable state with checkpointing, autoscaling, and both SQL and code (Java, Python) APIs.|
|E2|Broker independence|State whether the processing offering consumes from and produces to any Kafka-protocol-compatible cluster, or only the vendor’s own. Cross-vendor topologies must be supported or the constraint disclosed.|
|E3|Operability for a lean team|The platform team has no incumbent Flink or Spark expertise. Describe the operating model (fully managed versus customer-operated), the skills it demands, and the professional-services and training path to production.|
|E4|Tokenization in stream|Support for calling an external tokenization service in-line: rate limiting, retries, backpressure, audit, and guaranteed no-bypass semantics for sensitive fields.|

h3. F. Operations and self-service

||ID||Criterion||Requirement||
|F1|Infrastructure as code|A generally available, vendor-maintained Terraform provider and complete administrative APIs, suitable for GitOps; a Kubernetes operator where self-managed applies.|
|F2|Self-service provisioning|Declarative provisioning of topics, schemas, access, and connectors suitable for a golden-path onboarding portal; time-to-first-event in minutes; quotas and tenant isolation to contain noisy neighbours.|
|F3|Observability|Metrics export (Prometheus or OpenTelemetry), consumer-lag and end-to-end latency visibility, and per-topic dashboards and alerting integration.|
|F4|Support|24×7 enterprise support with priority-1 response of 30 minutes or better, named technical account management, and professional services for migration and build.|

h3. G. Migration and coexistence

||ID||Criterion||Requirement||
|G1|Migration from the current estate|Tooling and a proven pattern for migrating from self-managed Confluent Platform: topic and data replication, consumer-offset translation, schema migration, and phased per-topic cutover with dual-run. State expected parallel-run costs.|
|G2|Coexistence|Operate alongside the current estate for twelve months or longer, with bidirectional replication where required.|

h3. H. Total cost of ownership

||ID||Criterion||Requirement||
|H1|Three-year TCO|A complete three-year TCO at current and 10× volume, itemized as: licensing and consumption fees; cloud infrastructure (compute, storage, and inter-zone and cross-region replication and egress traffic, separately stated); one-time professional-services and migration cost; and the engineering headcount required to operate the proposed deployment model, by role and FTE count. h2. 5. Potential considerations for the target state

Known conditions that shape the target state; respond with these in mind.

# *Deployment model spectrum.* Fully managed, bring-your-own-cloud (data plane in our accounts), and modernized self-managed (Kubernetes-based) models are all admissible. Each is judged on the same criteria, including the engineering headcount it demands.
# *ZooKeeper retirement.* Apache Kafka 4.x removed ZooKeeper entirely; any proposed target state must be KRaft-based. ZooKeeper-dependent architectures are not acceptable.
# *Replication economics.* Inter-zone replication and cross-region traffic are first-order cost drivers at our volume. State how the architecture reduces these costs today, and the roadmap position on object-storage-backed (diskless) topics, now an accepted Kafka community direction.
# *Two clouds.* AWS (eu-west-1) is primary and Google Cloud parity is required for the target state. Single-cloud proposals must state the second-cloud path and its timeline.
# *Tokenization service reach.* Private connectivity from the platform and its processing tier to the enterprise tokenization service is a filtering constraint and is validated early in the evaluation, before scoring completes.
# *Compliance cadence.* Any new component entering the cardholder data environment triggers PCI re-attestation. Proposals should minimize the environment’s scope and state the attestation evidence they contribute.
# *Sink-engine independence.* Delivery of topics into Apache Iceberg is being evaluated as a separate decision. Platform proposals must stand on their own without requiring a proprietary sink path.
# *Vendor and ecosystem stability.* Ownership changes across the streaming ecosystem are an active consideration. Disclose any material change of control and provide multi-year roadmap commitments for every component proposed.

h2. 6. Response guidance

Respond to each criterion with a capability statement and supporting evidence: benchmark results with method, SLA documents, compliance attestations, and reference architectures from payments or financial-services deployments at comparable scale. Distinguish clearly between what is generally available and what is on the roadmap. The group H total cost of ownership must accompany the response, with assumptions stated. Shortlisted platforms proceed to a structured proof of concept, including validation of private connectivity to the tokenization service, before a decision targeted for October 2026.
